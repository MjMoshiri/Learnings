# Lecture 9: Ethology

## The gist

- Core idea: a lot of behavior is innate, species-specific programs, fired by simple cues and run open-loop. You find them by watching animals in their own world, not in a Skinner box.
- Ethology (Lorenz, Tinbergen, von Frisch, 1973 Nobel) was a reaction to behaviorism. Behaviorists treated species as interchangeable learning machines conditioned in the lab. Ethologists said behavior evolved for a specific niche and has to be studied there. Watch first, theorize later.
- Fixed action patterns (FAPs): innate, stereotyped sequences. No learning needed, practice polishes them, and once triggered they run to completion.
  - Squirrel raised on a liquid diet, never seeing a nut: hand it one and it cracks it, or runs the full burying routine on a bare floor.
  - Greylag goose: take the egg away mid-roll and she finishes the motion on nothing. The program ignores whether the input is still there.
- Releasing stimuli: a FAP is triggered by one simple feature, not the whole scene.
  - Male sticklebacks attack a crude model with a red belly and ignore a realistic fish without one.
  - Herring gull chicks peck the red spot on the parent's beak to get fed.
- Supernormal stimuli: exaggerate the releaser and the response beats the real thing. A stick with extra red stripes beats a gull's beak. Birds try to incubate giant fake eggs over their own.
- Humans run on releasers too. Baby schema (big head, big eyes, round face) triggers caretaking, and Mickey Mouse got more baby-faced over the decades.
- Human FAP candidates: universal facial expressions (Ekman), smiling in kids born blind, contagious yawning, infant grasp and rooting reflexes.
- Imprinting: a time-locked window where a gosling bonds to the first moving thing it sees. Lorenz's goslings followed him.
- Bee waggle dance (von Frisch): angle to vertical encodes direction relative to the sun, duration encodes distance. Symbolic communication in an insect.
- Neuroethology: find the neurons behind the releaser. The frog retina has "bug detector" cells tuned to small moving dark spots. The circuit mirrors the ecological need.

## Makes you think

The goose finishing an egg roll with no egg is the cleanest picture of how much behavior is open-loop. A trigger fires, a program runs, feedback barely matters. That's humbling, because a lot of what feels like choice in humans probably works the same way. Cuteness, lipstick, junk food, and cartoon eyes are supernormal stimuli exploiting releasers that evolved for something smaller.

Ethology's method matters too. Behaviorism assumed a universal learner and got clean lab data that missed what animals actually do. Ethology assumed each species solves its own problem and had to go watch. Same tension in ML: generic learners vs. the right inductive bias for the domain.
