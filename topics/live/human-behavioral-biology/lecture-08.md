# Lecture 8: Behavioral Genetics II

## The gist

- Core idea: "how much of this trait is genetic?" has no fixed answer. Genes and environment don't split 60/40. They interact, and the effect of one depends on the other.
- Heritability is not how genetic a trait is. It's the share of variation in a trait, in one population and one environment, that tracks genetic variation. Five fingers is fully genetic with near-zero heritability, since what variation exists comes from accidents.
- Heritability moves with the environment. Uniform environment, genes explain more of what's left. Varied environment, less. Turkheimer: IQ heritability is high in affluent families and near zero in poor ones.
- Twin and adoption estimates run high. Identical twins get treated more alike than fraternal twins. All twins share a womb, so prenatal environment gets counted as genetic. Adoption agencies place kids in homes like the birth family.
- Norm of reaction: a genotype is not one phenotype but a range of them across environments. Two genotypes can swap rank when conditions change.
  - Maze-bright vs. maze-dull rats (Cooper and Zubek): the gap vanishes in enriched and in impoverished environments. It only exists in the standard lab cage.
  - Crabbe: same mouse strains, same protocols, three labs, different results.
  - PKU: one gene defect causes severe cognitive impairment, and a low-phenylalanine diet mostly prevents it.
- Gene × environment in humans (Caspi): low-activity MAO-A predicts antisocial behavior only in men abused as children. The short serotonin transporter allele predicts depression only after heavy life stress.
- Genes mostly set how sensitive you are to the environment you land in. Vulnerability, not destiny.

## Makes you think

"Genes vs. environment, what's the split?" The split changes with the population and the environment you measure it in. MAO-A without abuse does nothing obvious. Abuse without the variant does less. The interesting part lives only in the product term, and a percentage can't hold it.

The genome is a set of if-then clauses. Behavioral genetics is what happens when you try to measure the "then" while pretending the "if" doesn't exist.
